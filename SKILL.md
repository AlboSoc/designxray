# Design X-Ray

Use this skill when the user asks to reverse-engineer, inspect, decode, analyse, or “X-ray” the design of an object, scene, product, interior, mechanism, interface, or other designed system.

Read `references/method.md` for the full reasoning method and `references/output-format.md` for response structure.

## Core objective

Move beyond recognition and description. Reveal the decisions, constraints, trade-offs, and opportunities embodied in visible details.

## Procedure

1. **Orient**
   - Give a brief literal description of the scene or object.
   - Note uncertainty, occlusion, poor lighting, or missing views.

2. **Select**
   - Choose 4–8 informative details.
   - Prefer easily overlooked features, interfaces between parts, signs of manufacture, wear, repair, adjustment, standardisation, or compromise.
   - Avoid spending the entire response on the most visually obvious object.

3. **Analyse each detail using PPPP**
   - **Purpose:** What function or human need might it serve?
   - **Pressures:** What constraints or forces plausibly produced this form?
   - **Perils:** What failure modes, hazards, frustrations, or unintended effects follow?
   - **Possibilities:** What alternatives, redesigns, experiments, or transferable ideas arise?

4. **Calibrate claims**
   - Mark direct observations as observations.
   - Phrase causal explanations as inference unless documented.
   - Label more adventurous ideas as speculation.
   - Offer competing explanations when evidence is ambiguous.

5. **Synthesize**
   - Identify patterns across the selected details.
   - State the scene’s dominant design tensions or strategies.
   - End with a one-sentence design thesis.

## Behaviour

- Be concrete and feature-specific.
- Explain mechanisms and trade-offs.
- Prefer surprising but defensible observations over exhaustive cataloguing.
- Do not merely praise or criticise aesthetics.
- Do not invent provenance, materials, dimensions, or designer intent.
- Treat wear, dirt, repairs, and improvised modifications as evidence of use.
- Relate local features to larger systems where appropriate: maintenance, staffing, regulation, logistics, accessibility, economics, social behaviour, and manufacturing.

## Modes

Infer depth from the request:

- **Quick:** 3–4 features, brief PPPP notes.
- **Standard:** 5–7 features plus synthesis.
- **Deep:** 8 or more features, competing hypotheses, interactions, and redesign opportunities.
