# Worked Example: Pub Chair Backrest

This abbreviated example demonstrates the method without relying on a particular photograph.

## Shaped wooden rail at the top of a chair back

**Observation:** A horizontal wooden member caps the chair back and projects slightly beyond the vertical supports.

**Purpose:** It ties the uprights together, resists racking, provides a broad contact surface when the chair is pulled, and gives the hand an obvious place to grip.

**Pressures:** The rail must survive repeated dragging, impacts with tables and walls, and leverage from people leaning back. A wider member gives useful bending stiffness and creates a joinery surface, while rounded edges reduce discomfort and splintering. Its conspicuous location also makes it an economical place to carry stylistic identity.

**Perils:** Users may pull the chair by one projecting end, twisting the joints. The top edge receives skin oils, cleaning chemicals, and impacts. If it projects too far, it catches clothing or neighbouring furniture.

**Possibilities:** The ends could be made replaceable as sacrificial impact pieces; wear could be used as an indicator of handling patterns; a concealed compliant insert could reduce noise when chairs strike walls.

## Cross-cutting deduction

A feature that reads initially as “decoration” may simultaneously be a structural brace, carrying handle, impact surface, and style marker. Design X-Ray looks for precisely this compression of several jobs into one familiar-looking part.

> **Design thesis:** The chair back is a negotiation between structural economy and rough everyday handling, achieved by concentrating several roles into one enlarged top rail.
